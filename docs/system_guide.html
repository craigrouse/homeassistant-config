🚨 Break glass — if you're reading this because something's wrong

Golden rule: almost everything in this house fails safe if you simply turn the automations off. Lights, heating and the alarm all work manually. The only subsystem that actively moves money and energy around is the Powerwall scheduler — read the box below before touching it.

Stop the energy system safely

  1. Turn on the helper input_boolean.power_hour_active. This is the global freeze flag: the Daily Scheduler, watchdogs and slot automations all stand down while it's on.
  2. In the Tesla app → Powerwall → Settings, set the mode to Self-Powered. The house now just uses solar + battery like a normal install, no tariff games.
  3. That's it. The house has mains power regardless; the batteries keep ~27 kWh in reserve and the backup gateway handles power cuts automatically.

To resume: turn power_hour_active back off and run the automation "Powerwall: Daily Scheduler" manually once (Settings → Automations → ⋮ → Run). It re-establishes the correct tariff and mode for the current time of day.

EnergyPowerwall arbitrage — the crown jewels

The house earns money by importing at 6.9p overnight and exporting at 12p during the day, cycling the Powerwalls hard (PW3s tolerate this well; occasional full drains are deliberate for BMS calibration). Solar charges the batteries losslessly (DC-coupled), so conversion cost only applies to AC import/export.

Architecture: one writer, many delegates

Powerwall: Daily Scheduler is the single source of truth. It is the only automation that pushes tariff plans to the Powerwall (via teslemetry.time_of_use). The trick: it feeds Tesla's optimiser fake tariffs — a fake sky-high sell price makes the battery dump to grid; fake cheap windows make it charge. Every other energy automation (slot delegate, watchdogs, session controllers) delegates back to the scheduler with automation.trigger + skip_condition: false rather than pushing tariffs themselves — this "single TOU writer" rule is what keeps the system from fighting itself. If you ever add an energy automation, follow that pattern.

Global freeze flag: input_boolean.power_hour_active — when on, the scheduler and watchdogs do nothing. Sessions and manual overrides use it to take control cleanly.

Paid vs free sessions: both arrive on the same Octopus calendars with identical wording. The only discriminator is octopoints_per_kwh on the joined event: 0 = free electricity session (fill the battery), >0 = paid saving session (dump the battery). Two template binary sensors — binary_sensor.octopus_paid_saving_session_active and binary_sensor.octopus_free_session_active — encode this and everything gates on them. Kill switch for the automated free-session dump: input_boolean.free_session_auto_dump.
Never export while a car is charging — the meter nets instantaneously, so exported energy just cancels the car's cheap import. The free-session pre-dump pauses both cars' smart charging for exactly this reason and the Power Hour controller re-enables it.

EnergyEV charging & slot farming

The shadow charge system

Normal nights: the plugged-in car charges to its 80% Octopus target by 05:30. When more range is wanted for the day, the shadow system tops it up on cheap morning slots instead of paying day rate:

  1. Evening: set input_datetime.tesla_shadow_ready_by (a half-hour time, e.g. 10:30 — when you want the car ready), set input_number.tesla_desired_soc (the shadow limit, e.g. 100%), and arm input_boolean.home_for_morning.
  2. Morning: "EV: Morning Charge Extension" fires with charge-rate-aware lead time before the shadow time (Tesla ~9.8%/h, Renault ~14.2%/h, +20 min buffer), raises the Octopus charge target to the shadow limit and moves the ready-by to the shadow time. Octopus grants 6.9p slots to close the gap — and each slot lets the Powerwall grid-charge cheaply too.
  3. Midday: everything resets — ready-by to 05:30, charge target back to 80% (only if the shadow flow raised it), arm toggle cleared. The arm survives overnight; only midday clears it.

Car detection on the shared Wall Connector: connector occupied + binary_sensor.meilin_charge_cable on = Tesla; occupied + cable off = Renault 5.

Safety responses

  • Smoke/CO (Nest Protect, hallway): Ring alarm triggers and every light in and outside the house goes to full brightness. The door deliberately does not unlock.
  • Water leak: flood sensors push an alert.
  • Door opened while armed: announced through the house.
  • Garage door (detached, Ring contact sensor): alerts if open at 22:00, open when the house empties, or open 2 hours straight. Notify-only — no motorised opener.
  • Nuki jam alerts, evening lock-status summary, entry-code auto-reset, and a Ring integration self-reload if it drops offline.

LightsLighting

The biggest group (~45 automations), but the philosophy is simple: rooms light themselves on schedules, motion and buttons; several sweeps turn things off; nobody should ever think about it. Floor-targeted actions (floor_id: upstairs etc.) mean new bulbs are covered automatically once assigned to a room.

On

  • Evening schedules: lounge, kitchen, bedroom, ensuite, upstairs (kids' rooms) come on around dusk.
  • Morning: kitchen counter lights + radio; lounge.
  • Motion/doors: WC, kids bathroom, study (only when dark — an illuminance threshold gates it), garage on car arrival, garden light on door open, loft light on hatch open.
  • Buttons: every bathroom/bedroom has physical button controls; those automations are named "Room: Button Controls".

ClimateHeating & hot water

Design decision: the heat pump is not scheduled. The Daikin Altherma runs at a constant low temperature all winter and just keeps going — do not build or restore a heating schedule. Its relevance to automation is load: on the coldest days it draws up to 30 kWh, which is why the morning slot-farming (and the planned cold-day battery strategy) matter — keeping the batteries topped with 6.9p energy is what pays for the heating.

  • Hot water (water_heater.altherma): morning heat via heat_pump mode; auto-off above 55°C; performance mode = immersion boost, used automatically during free electricity sessions.
  • ⚠️ The Altherma does not support "set temperature" — only operation-mode changes (off / heat_pump / performance). A set_temperature call errors and can abort an automation sequence. Historic bug; don't reintroduce it.
  • Supporting cast: dehumidifier stall control, heated clothes rack off at off-peak end, kids' fans off overnight, heated blanket timer, extractor fans tied to bathroom lights.

Holiday behaviour

  • Presence simulation: lounge + kitchen lights come on somewhere in 21:00–21:30 (random jitter nightly) and the whole house goes dark somewhere in 22:45–23:15 — looks lived-in, never identical two nights running. Outside lights stay motion-driven.
  • Energy: business as usual — the batteries earn all week.
  • Hot water: handled per-trip (one-shot automations, e.g. off on departure, reheat cheaply before return). Delete each one-shot after the trip.

Presence reliability

Jade Location Stale Watchdog: Nothing OS throttles the HA app's background location — the phone keeps reporting but replays a cached fix (signature: GPS accuracy pinned at exactly 100, frozen coordinates), so Jade can show "home" while out. When Craig is away and her tracker serves accuracy-100 fixes for 30+ minutes, the watchdog sends the app's silent request_location_update command push to force a real fix, and escalates to Craig's phone at 90 minutes. Manual fix on the phone: open Google Maps for a fresh fix, or open the HA app.

SoftenerWater softener salt tracking

The Kinetico Kube is purely mechanical, so salt level is tracked with a human-in-the-loop model calibrated by the Aquaro water meter:

  • Sunday 10:00 (when home; else next arrival once 7+ days overdue): a push asks for the level — buttons 100% / 75% / Lower… (then 50% / 25% / Empty).
  • Each answer teaches the model how many litres of water burn 1% of salt (learned rate in input_number.salt_litres_per_percent). Answer honestly and coarsely: if it looks the same as last week, answer the same number — repeats are free and keep the measurement window accumulating for one high-quality sample when a visible drop finally happens. Refill answers (higher than before) reset the baseline without corrupting the learning.
  • sensor.salt_level_live interpolates downward between check-ins from actual water flow; sensor.salt_days_remaining converts at 550 L/day.
  • Alerts at 15% ("buy salt") and 5% ("refill now") — both carry a Refilled to 100% button, and any low answer leaves a sticky refill button in the notification tray.

⚠️ Known pitfalls (learned the hard way)

  • Teslemetry is version-pinned. The HACS custom Teslemetry v6.0.15 requires a newer HA core than 2026.8.3 and silently breaks every tariff push (DeviceRegistry … include_child_devices error in traces). Pinned at v6.0.3, broken version skipped in HA updates. Unpin only after a core upgrade or a fixed release. Symptom if it recurs: exports/dumps "arm" but the Tesla-side tariff never changes.
  • A failed tariff push fails silently. The Powerwall just keeps its old plan. Always check the automation trace, not the dashboard, when export behaviour looks wrong.
  • automation.trigger defaults to bypassing conditions. Every delegate call in this system passes skip_condition: false deliberately. Omitting it caused a whole class of session-clobbering bugs once — keep the pattern.
  • Altherma: operation-mode only, never set_temperature (see Climate).
  • Nothing Phone location staleness (see Presence) — accuracy pinned at exactly 100 = cached fix.
  • Octopus reverts manual charge-target changes within about an hour — automate the change or make it in the Octopus app.
  • UniFi tracker allowlist: the UniFi integration is restricted to family devices. Adding a device to presence tracking means adding it to the allowlist in the integration options, or you'll re-flood HA with hundreds of MAC-randomised guests.
  • The energy system runs during holidays by design. holiday_mode does not pause it — don't "fix" that.
  • Free-session import cap: guard trips at 15 kWh against the 16 kWh fair-use limit (~1 kWh meter-lag margin). Don't raise it casually.
